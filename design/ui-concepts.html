Design directions · for selection

CardVault, three ways.

Three complete visual directions for the local, encrypted card vault. Same product, same privacy rule — the home screen never shows a number, details stay masked until you reveal them — three different material worlds. Every screen below is mocked at full UI fidelity; all card data is synthetic sample content.

Open each direction, then tell me “A”, “B” or “C”. I’ll rebuild every Avalonia view in the chosen world.

A · Readout B · Machine Room C · Atelier
DIRECTION A

Readout

A seven-segment display family — bedside clock, gas-station totem, scoreboard. Numbers are the whole product, so absence is drawn as deliberately as light: every unlit segment exists. The vault is a rack of masked cells; the lock is an unset clock blinking its colon; revealing a card is segments igniting.

MaterialMatte near-black glass, lit LED segments, ghost (unlit) cells.
Palette
TypeSeven-segment glyphs for every number & code · Space Grotesk for labels/prose.
MotionNothing slides. Every state is an instant segment swap; the locked clock blinks.
RiskA museum piece more than a daily app unless the mask logic feels functional — but the product is masked numbers.

DIRECTION B

Machine Room

A normalled jackfield: the schedule taped inside a machine-room door, amber lanes over black glass, everything tied to its default. The vault is normalled — its normal is “locked”. Unlocking plugs the line; breaking a card’s link is disabling or deleting it. No colour carries state — the line does.

MaterialBlack glass, hairline rules, one signal amber, numbered lanes that never reflow.
Palette
TypeOne condensed grotesque (Barlow Condensed), small-cap legends, rank by inversion on an amber plate.
StateLink lines: solid = live · dashed gap = disabled · doubled = selected · open ring = destructive.
RiskMachine-room darkness can feel cold outside its nerd context; the patch language needs to stay legible to a non-engineer.

DIRECTION C

Atelier

A single uncut sheet of washi — vermilion face, sumi ink, kozo fiber, one gold dot marking the active step. Paper is the primitive: cards are printed plates, the home is a folio, revealing a card opens its crease. Print-craft quiet: no glow, no glass, just paper, ink and one trusting fold.

MaterialWashi paper, sumi ink, crease-white fold lines, one gold dot; layered sheet shadows.
Palette
TypeZen Kaku Gothic for interface copy · Shippori Mincho for card names and headings.
MotionOne authored moment: the reveal is a crease opening, not a fade. Everything else stays still.
RiskThe fold poetry can slow a quick grab; creases stay a reveal-only device, never a navigation metaphor.

Also weighed
A pill-elbow starship console (playful, pastel-on-black) and a zoo-guide map (flat lobes) were fused, then declined — both risked reading as décor rather than a vault. The console’s instant colour-swap discipline was folded into Readout; the map’s overshoot was not taken anywhere.
What’s shared
The privacy rule is identical in all three: the home surface never carries a number. Every direction keeps live validation, reveal-to-copy, auto-lock, and encrypted export. Settings and dialogs follow from the world’s own grammar.
